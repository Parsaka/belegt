Climate figures of German companies, checked.

Belegt links the verified CO₂ emissions of every German installation in the EU Emissions Trading System to the corporate group that owns it, reads the Scope 1–3 figures that 37 DAX and MDAX groups publish in their annual reports, and compares the two. A multi-tenant assistant answers questions about the reports with a page citation for every claim, and it never shows one tenant another tenant's documents.

Why

A company's climate figure is a claim. Is there anything independent to check it against?

Since the CSRD, large German groups publish their greenhouse-gas emissions in an audited sustainability statement. Those figures sit in PDFs of 300 to 500 pages, in tables whose layout differs from company to company. And there is little that a reader can easily compare them with.

THE INDEPENDENT DATA EXISTS

EU ETS verified emissions

Every power plant, steelworks and chemical site in the EU Emissions Trading System reports its CO₂ each year, and an accredited verifier checks it. Germany has 2,195 stationary installations in the 2025 registry file.

BUT IT IS PER SITE

No link to the group

The registry names the operator of a site, often a small subsidiary, such as a Kraftwerk GmbH. Only 76 of the 2,874 German operators give an LEI. To learn which group a site belonged to in a given year, you need ownership data and entity resolution.

AND THE CLAIMS ARE IN PDFS

Scope figures in free-form tables

"Gross Scope 1 emissions 15.552 15.216 …" means 15.552 million tonnes, but only if you know the header is in Mt, which column is 2024, and that the row is the group total and not a segment.

The goal. I wanted a pipeline that joins these three things: who emits (verified, per site), who owns them (per year), and what the owner says (per page). It should be honest about what it cannot match, cite its source for every figure, and run on a student budget. I also built it to close specific gaps that German data-engineering job ads ask for: GCP and BigQuery, Kubernetes, a semantic layer, dimensional modelling beyond the basics (SCD2, bridge tables, entity resolution), and RAG that can be trusted.

How it works

Four questions, one warehouse

Each question is a layer of the platform, and each layer has its own measurement.

01 · WHO EMITS?

Verified emissions per installation

The EU Union Registry xlsx files, 2008 to 2025, loaded idempotently and checksummed in a manifest. The result is 43,272 German account-years.

02 · WHO OWNS THEM?

Operator → legal entity → group

Entity resolution against 310,087 GLEIF entities uses the LEI, then the register number, then the name. A time-bounded ownership bridge then rolls each entity up to its ultimate parent in that year.

03 · WHAT DO THEY SAY?

Scope 1–3 from 37 reports

A Kubernetes Indexed Job parses one PDF per pod. A rule-based extractor finds the E1-6 table, reads its header years and units, and stores the figure with its page and table line.

04 · DO THEY AGREE?

Reported vs verified, and questions

A consistency mart compares the reported Scope 1 with the group's ETS sum. A tenant-isolated assistant answers questions from the reports, with page citations, or declines.

Open data in, cited answers out

GitHub Actions orchestrates everything through Workload Identity Federation, so no service-account key exists anywhere. Terraform owns every resource, and the cost guards were the first thing it built.

Follow one number

From a table row on page 54 to a checked figure

BASF's 2024 report, with the real row as the PDF text layer gives it.

Find the climate pages

The report has hundreds of pages. Only pages that mention a scope, GHG/THG or the ESRS datapoint E1-6 are read further.

Read the table, not the line

The extractor reconstructs the header (years, base year, change columns, footnote digits glued to years) and the unit (t, kt, "1,000 tonnes", Mt CO₂e). Then it reads the value in the reporting-year column.

Gross Scope 1 emissions 15.552 15.216 15.562 – 0% … → scope1 = 15,552,000 t · page 54

Store it with its evidence

fct_reported_metric keeps the value, the page and the table line (at most 200 characters, so it stays a quote). A reviewed value can override it through a seed, and the source is recorded.

Compare with what was verified

mart_scope1_consistency joins the figure with the ETS sum of every installation that the group owned in that year. ETS covers only EU sites, so a reported figure above the ETS sum is normal. Only an ETS sum above the reported figure (beyond 5 %) is flagged as a contradiction.

Answer questions about it

"What were BASF's gross Scope 1 GHG emissions in 2024?" searches only BASF's report pages that the asking tenant may read. The answer quotes one row and cites its chunk, or the assistant declines.

Why the group, and why "in that year"

An installation's operator is often a subsidiary that changed hands. GLEIF publishes who the direct and ultimate parents are, with dates. Belegt keeps them as a time-bounded bridge:

bridge_ownership child_lei parent_lei kind valid_from valid_to <operator> <operator> self … … <operator> <group> ultimate 2019-… 2023-…

"Which group owned this plant in 2022?" is then a join on the year end, not on today's parent. A dbt test makes sure the periods never overlap, and another checks that each group's total equals the sum of the facts attributed to it.

Every entity also gets a self edge, so an operator without a parent in GLEIF is its own group and its tonnage is not lost.

A known limit, shown rather than hidden. Hüttenwerke Krupp Mannesmann has three shareholders and no consolidating parent in GLEIF, so it stays its own group (6.5 Mt in 2025). Splitting it pro rata would need ownership shares, which GLEIF Level 2 does not carry.

Deep dives

The hard parts, and how each was measured

1 · Entity resolution with a review queue

The registry gives a name and usually a German commercial register number, such as HRB 6000 Amtsgericht Ludwigshafen. GLEIF stores the same number in registeredAs. The matcher tries the most reliable evidence first, and it never decides silently when the evidence is weak.

Evidence, in order

  1. The LEI in the registry is accepted directly, but only 76 German operators give one.
  2. A register number equal to GLEIF registeredAs with a plausible name is accepted. The same number with another name goes to review, because numbers repeat across courts: HRB 6000 belongs to five GLEIF entities.
  3. A normalised name (umlauts and legal forms removed): an exact match is accepted, and a partial match goes to review.
  4. The EEA parent company is used only where nothing else matched.

Human decisions are a seed (entity_overrides.csv), so a re-run can never undo them.

2 · Extraction from 37 real reports, with a held-out test split

I labelled 148 figures by hand: 37 reports × Scope 1, Scope 2 location-based, Scope 2 market-based and Scope 3. Each label has the value, the pages and the printed table line. A pick counts as correct only within 0.5 % of the label and on a labelled page. An empty label means the report states no such figure, so any pick there counts as an error.

How the rules were changed

Rules changed only for failures in the dev split, and each change came with a unit test built from the real row: a base-year column without a year, a change column (2024/2023), "1,000 tonnes" in a row label, footnote digits glued to header years, column titles between header lines, wrapped rows, and Konzern/Summe total rows.

None of the 22 test reports changed a rule. Their precision still rose from 61.2 % to 80.8 %, so the fixes generalise rather than memorise. One report whose header shaped a rule was moved from test to dev, and the method page says so.

3 · A multi-tenant assistant that cites or declines

The reports are split between two tenants: energy and materials, and mobility, logistics and technology. An answer is worth something only if a reader can check it, and only if a tenant can never see a document that it does not own. Both are enforced outside the model.

19 of 31

questions about a company's Scope 1 figure answered with the printed figure and a citation on a labelled page

31 of 31

questions declined when the other tenant asks about the same report

0 leaks

citations of a report that the asking tenant may not read, over 4,915 chunks from 37 reports

Isolation is a filter, not a prompt

  • The tenant comes from the bearer token and never from the request body.
  • The VECTOR_SEARCH base table is filtered on the tenant before the nearest-neighbour search, and BigQuery row access policies enforce the same rule for each tenant's service account.
  • All GHG tables look alike, so a company named in the question narrows the search to that company's reports. This filter runs in the same query and is never a way around the tenant filter.

Every sentence needs a citation

  • Chunks never cross a page and keep table rows whole, so a citation names one page.
  • After generation, the assistant declines with a fixed sentence when a sentence has no citation, or when it cites a chunk that was not retrieved.
  • Text inside the sources is data. A test plants an instruction in a document and checks that the citation check catches it.
  • A model-free extractive reader runs in CI and when the LLM spend cap is hit, so the evaluation needs no paid API.

The test cases are generated from the extraction labels, and a test keeps the two in sync. All 12 misses come from the model-free reader: a citation on another page that also mentions Scope 1, the right report but the wrong row, or a decline. The 0 leaks hold whatever the reader is, because they come from the filters and the citation check. The method and every miss are on the method page.

4 · A platform with a hard 1 EUR ceiling

The budget was a design input, not an afterthought. The cost guards were the first Terraform that was applied, and each one is tested.

LAYERED GUARDS
  • Budget alerts at 0.25, 0.50 and 1.00 EUR.
  • A custom BigQuery quota of 20 GiB a day, a hard stop that also protects the public dashboard.
  • Cloud Run with min 0 and max 1 instance, a rate limit and a demo token.
  • A billing kill switch at 0.60 EUR: budget → Pub/Sub → function → detach billing.
A DRILL, NOT A HOPE

After every Terraform apply, a workflow sends a marked budget message and checks the function's verdict. It stops just before the detach.

The drill found two real bugs. The function was not registered as a CloudEvent function, and its identity lacked resourcemanager.projects.get. Either bug would have left billing attached. Both are fixed, and the drill now reports WOULD DETACH.

TWO RUNTIMES, ONE IMAGE

GKE Autopilot runs the report job during the 90-day trial, and a Cloud Run job runs it afterwards. The same container image runs on both. The Helm charts are tested on kind in CI, so the Kubernetes path stays valid after the cluster is gone.

The measured result is 37 of 37 reports parsed in parallel on GKE, with no failed task.

Decisions

One tool per job, and what I chose not to build

Each technology here has a role that no other tool in the stack already plays. The trade-offs are written down as architecture decision records.

BigQuerySnowflakea vector DB

One warehouse, also the vector store

Two warehouses would only duplicate models. The BigQuery free tier fits the budget, and VECTOR_SEARCH together with row access policies removes a separate vector database. The engine-specific SQL lives in dbt macros, so the models remain portable. ADR 0001

GKE in the trialCloud Run afterCloud SQLDagster

Build in the trial, run on the free tier

Run continuously, GKE, Looker, Cloud SQL and Dagster would cost about 80 USD a month. GKE runs only during the trial, on the same image as the later Cloud Run job. Cloud SQL and Dagster were dropped before they were built: they would have duplicated BigQuery and GitHub Actions. ADR 0002

filter firstcitation checkabstain

Cite or decline, isolate before retrieval

An answer that is fluent but unsupported is worse than none. The pipeline rejects some correct but uncited answers on purpose. Tenant isolation is enforced by the query and by row access policies, never by the prompt. ADR 0003

LookML in CILooker StudioLooker instance

A semantic layer without a paid instance

Google granted this project a Looker trial quota of 0. The LookML stays in the repo, and a CI test checks it against the dbt build, so it is ready if an instance becomes available. Looker Studio is the public dashboard. ADR 0004

rules + labelsLLM extraction first

Measure a transparent baseline first

The extractor is deterministic, and every pick carries its page and line, so a wrong figure is visible and can be overridden. With 148 labels and a held-out split, any later model can be compared with the same numbers rather than judged on impressions.

links + quotesPDFs in Git

Respect the copyright of the reports

The PDFs live only in a private bucket and on the CI runner. Git holds the links, the page numbers and table lines of at most 200 characters. Every dataset has its licence in data-sources.md, and a test checks that each one is also registered in code.

Honest limits

What it does not do yet

  • Extraction recall on unseen reports is 62.7 %. Multi-entity tables and figures that sit two lines below their label still fail. A layout-aware parser or an LLM reader would be measured against the same labels.
  • The assistant's reader is model-free. Its 12 misses come from which page or row it quotes. No LLM reader was part of the measured run, so no LLM numbers are claimed.
  • ETS covers only installations in Germany here. A group's ETS share of its global Scope 1 can be small (Heidelberg Materials: 4.9 %) without any contradiction, which is why only ETS above reported is flagged.
  • Most map points are approximate. Only 49 installations have an exact EEA point. The rest sit at their contact postcode, and the map says so.
  • Joint ventures are not split. GLEIF Level 2 has no ownership shares, so a site with several shareholders stays its own group.
  • GKE and the trial are temporary. After the trial, the report job moves to Cloud Run and everything else stays on the free tier.
Run it

Runs locally in minutes, with no cloud account

The same dbt models run on DuckDB over trimmed real fixtures. That is also how this site's dbt docs are built.

# Python 3.11+
pip install -e ".[dev,load,dbt]"
pytest                      # 185 tests, no network
belegt sources              # every dataset with its licence

# warehouse on DuckDB from the fixtures
belegt load --source tests/fixtures --db data/belegt.duckdb
dbt build --project-dir dbt --profiles-dir dbt

Engineering practice

  • The src/ layout, with ruff, mypy and pytest in CI. The fixtures are trimmed real files.
  • Idempotent writes: re-runs overwrite, and a manifest keeps the checksums.
  • Keyless CI/CD with Workload Identity Federation, and no secrets in Git.
  • Small pull requests per phase, each with its measured result.
  • An informative User-Agent on every download.
